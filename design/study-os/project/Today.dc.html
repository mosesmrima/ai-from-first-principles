Linear Algebra Done Right · §5.A

Invariant subspaces

Suppose T is an operator on V. A subspace U of V is invariant under T when Tu lands back inside U for every u in U — the operator never carries U outside itself.

The one-dimensional invariant subspaces are exactly the spans of eigenvectors: T(λv) = λ(Tv) stays on the line through v. This is where Av = λv stops being a formula and becomes geometry.
